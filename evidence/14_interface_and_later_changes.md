# E14 — Interface restrictions and later advertisement appeals

**Sources:** original screenshots from 9–10, 13 and 18 October 2026; the sent support message of 10 October; selected Claude work-history records.

## What is directly visible

The early screenshots show Display-1 as “Removed”, with its advertisement ineligible/disapproved. A disabled editing control and an attempted ad-group removal are documented. The latter produces the exact message:

> Your account has been suspended. As a result, you can't perform certain actions.

That establishes a real practical obstacle at the time of capture. It does not identify what caused the suspension, nor prove that every possible action was permanently disabled. The more specific phrase “Can't perform action on removed campaign” appears in some later dossiers but was not verified as the exact text of this original screenshot.

## Subsequent developments

Original screenshots taken on 18 October show two advertisement-policy appeals dated that day, each “In progress”: “Destination not working”, with the reason “Made changes to comply with policy”, and “Moderately restricted”, with “Dispute decision”. Those are direct observations of submitted appeals.

Claude work notes from that evening report changing the ad's destination, business name, text and images to Bento Corp details. The policy screenshot confirms the appeal and the reason selected, not every claimed saved change. A separate screenshot says no ads match the active filters; it is not proof that the advertisement was deleted. No final result of those two ad appeals was identified in the reviewed evidence.

The public account therefore dates the blocked operations to 10 October and records the later actions. It does not say the account was permanently incapable of any edit or appeal.

## Other-account disclosure

In a sent message on 10 October, Pedro reports that another account appeared while he was checking an old business login after the suspension on 9 October. He says it was unused and that he cancelled it, and asks Google to confirm. A contemporaneous work history also records his statement that he cancelled it. The audit did not obtain a separate Google cancellation confirmation or a backend creation record. A browser-history visit timestamp is not conclusive proof of an account's creation time.

This disclosure is retained without publishing its identifiers. Its possible relevance to later reviews remains unresolved; it is not established as the cause of the initial suspension.
